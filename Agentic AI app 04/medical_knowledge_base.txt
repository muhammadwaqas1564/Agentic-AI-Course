CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASES
========================

Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
Symptoms: persistent headaches, dizziness, blurred vision, chest pain, shortness of breath, nosebleeds, palpitations
Risk Factors: obesity, high salt diet, smoking, alcohol, family history, age > 40, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, sedentary lifestyle
Recommended Tests: blood pressure monitoring, ECG, echocardiogram, blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine, lipid panel, urinalysis
Treatment: lifestyle modification, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, diuretics, beta-blockers
Priority: High if BP > 180/120 (hypertensive crisis), Medium otherwise

Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
Symptoms: chest pain (angina), shortness of breath, heart attack symptoms (crushing chest pain radiating to left arm/jaw), fatigue, nausea, sweating
Risk Factors: high LDL cholesterol, hypertension, diabetes, smoking, obesity, family history, male gender, age > 45 men / > 55 women
Recommended Tests: ECG, stress test, echocardiogram, coronary angiography, troponin levels, CK-MB, lipid panel, CRP
Treatment: statins, aspirin, beta-blockers, nitroglycerin, coronary bypass surgery, angioplasty
Priority: Critical if active chest pain, High otherwise

Heart Failure
Symptoms: shortness of breath (dyspnea), orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, leg swelling (edema), rapid weight gain, fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, persistent cough
Risk Factors: CAD, hypertension, diabetes, cardiomyopathy, valve disease, obesity, alcohol abuse
Recommended Tests: BNP/NT-proBNP, echocardiogram, chest X-ray, ECG, complete metabolic panel, complete blood count
Treatment: ACE inhibitors, ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics, aldosterone antagonists, cardiac resynchronization therapy
Priority: Critical if acute decompensation, High for chronic

Atrial Fibrillation
Symptoms: irregular heartbeat, palpitations, shortness of breath, dizziness, chest discomfort, fatigue
Risk Factors: age > 65, hypertension, heart failure, CAD, thyroid disease, sleep apnea, alcohol
Recommended Tests: ECG, Holter monitor, echocardiogram, thyroid function tests, electrolytes
Treatment: rate control, rhythm control, anticoagulation (warfarin, DOACs), cardioversion, ablation
Priority: High

RESPIRATORY DISEASES
====================

Pneumonia
Symptoms: fever > 38.5°C, productive cough, chest pain worsening with breathing, shortness of breath, fatigue, chills, rigors, decreased breath sounds
Risk Factors: age > 65, immunocompromised, COPD, diabetes, smoking, alcohol, recent hospitalization, influenza
Recommended Tests: chest X-ray, CBC with differential, blood cultures, sputum culture, procalcitonin, CRP, ABG (if severe)
Treatment: antibiotics (amoxicillin, azithromycin, fluoroquinolones), oxygen therapy, hospitalization if severe
Priority: High to Critical

COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)
Symptoms: chronic cough, excessive mucus, shortness of breath, wheezing, chest tightness, frequent respiratory infections
Risk Factors: smoking (primary), occupational dust/fumes, biomass fuel exposure, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, age > 40
Recommended Tests: spirometry (FEV1/FVC < 0.70), chest X-ray, CT chest, ABG, CBC, alpha-1 antitrypsin level
Treatment: bronchodilators (SABA, LABA), ICS, PDE4 inhibitors, oxygen therapy, pulmonary rehabilitation
Priority: High during exacerbation, Medium for stable

Asthma
Symptoms: episodic wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, cough (often nocturnal or with exercise), triggered by allergens/cold air/exercise
Risk Factors: family history, allergies, eczema, obesity, smoking, air pollution, occupational exposures
Recommended Tests: spirometry, peak flow monitoring, bronchodilator reversibility test, allergy testing (skin prick/RAST), FeNO
Treatment: SABA (salbutamol), ICS, LABA, leukotriene modifiers, biologics (severe), avoid triggers
Priority: Critical if severe attack, Medium for controlled

Pulmonary Embolism
Symptoms: sudden shortness of breath, pleuritic chest pain, hemoptysis, tachycardia, leg swelling/pain (DVT), hypoxia, syncope
Risk Factors: prolonged immobility, recent surgery, malignancy, previous DVT/PE, oral contraceptives, pregnancy, thrombophilia
Recommended Tests: D-dimer, CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA), V/Q scan, lower limb Doppler, ECG (S1Q3T3), echo
Treatment: anticoagulation (heparin, LMWH, DOACs), thrombolysis if massive, IVC filter
Priority: Critical

METABOLIC AND ENDOCRINE DISEASES
==================================

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
Symptoms: polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision, slow wound healing, recurrent infections, numbness/tingling in feet
Risk Factors: obesity (BMI > 30), family history, physical inactivity, age > 45, hypertension, dyslipidemia, gestational diabetes, PCOS, prediabetes
Recommended Tests: fasting plasma glucose (>= 126 mg/dL), HbA1c (>= 6.5%), 2-hour OGTT (>= 200 mg/dL), random glucose (>= 200 + symptoms), lipid panel, kidney function, urine microalbumin, foot exam, eye exam
Treatment: lifestyle modification, metformin, GLP-1 agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP4 inhibitors, sulfonylureas, insulin
Priority: High for uncontrolled, Medium for controlled

Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus
Symptoms: rapid onset polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, fatigue, DKA (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity breath, altered consciousness)
Risk Factors: family history, autoimmune diseases, viral infections
Recommended Tests: fasting glucose, HbA1c, C-peptide (low), anti-GAD antibodies, anti-islet cell antibodies, anti-insulin antibodies, urinalysis for ketones
Treatment: insulin (basal-bolus regimen), carbohydrate counting, glucose monitoring, CGM
Priority: Critical if DKA

Hypothyroidism
Symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, bradycardia, depression, cognitive slowing, menstrual irregularities
Risk Factors: Hashimoto's thyroiditis, previous thyroid surgery/radioiodine, female gender, age > 60, family history, autoimmune diseases
Recommended Tests: TSH (elevated), free T4 (low), anti-TPO antibodies, anti-thyroglobulin antibodies, CBC, lipid panel
Treatment: levothyroxine replacement, monitor TSH every 6-12 months
Priority: Medium

Hyperthyroidism / Graves' Disease
Symptoms: weight loss despite increased appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, palpitations, tremor, anxiety, diarrhea, exophthalmos (Graves'), neck swelling
Risk Factors: Graves' disease, toxic multinodular goiter, thyroid adenoma, female gender, family history, autoimmune diseases
Recommended Tests: TSH (suppressed), free T4 (elevated), free T3, TSH receptor antibodies, thyroid uptake scan, thyroid ultrasound
Treatment: anti-thyroid drugs (methimazole, PTU), radioiodine, thyroidectomy, beta-blockers for symptom control
Priority: High for thyroid storm, Medium otherwise

GASTROINTESTINAL DISEASES
===========================

Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)
Symptoms: heartburn (burning sensation in chest after eating), regurgitation of acid, dysphagia, chronic cough, hoarseness, dental erosion
Risk Factors: obesity, hiatal hernia, smoking, alcohol, fatty/spicy foods, caffeine, pregnancy, certain medications (NSAIDs, calcium channel blockers)
Recommended Tests: upper endoscopy (EGD), pH monitoring (24-hour), manometry, barium swallow, H. pylori testing
Treatment: PPIs, H2 blockers, antacids, lifestyle modification, surgical fundoplication (severe)
Priority: Low to Medium; High if Barrett's esophagus

Peptic Ulcer Disease
Symptoms: epigastric pain (burning/gnawing), nausea, vomiting, bloating, dark stools (melena), hematemesis, weight loss, pain relieved or worsened by food
Risk Factors: H. pylori infection (primary), NSAIDs/aspirin use, smoking, stress, alcohol, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
Recommended Tests: upper endoscopy, H. pylori testing (breath test, stool antigen, serology), CBC (for bleeding), gastrin levels
Treatment: H. pylori eradication (triple therapy), PPIs, H2 blockers, stop NSAIDs, bismuth subsalicylate
Priority: High if bleeding, Medium otherwise

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)
Symptoms: recurrent abdominal pain associated with defecation, change in bowel habits (diarrhea, constipation, or mixed), bloating, mucus in stool, no alarm features
Risk Factors: female gender, age < 50, history of GI infections, anxiety, depression, food sensitivities, family history
Recommended Tests: diagnosis of exclusion; CBC, CRP, ESR, fecal calprotectin, colonoscopy (if alarm features), celiac disease serology
Treatment: dietary modification (low FODMAP), antispasmodics, antidiarrheals (loperamide), laxatives, antidepressants (TCAs, SSRIs), rifaximin
Priority: Low to Medium

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)
Symptoms: chronic diarrhea (often bloody in UC), abdominal pain and cramping, weight loss, fatigue, fever, extraintestinal manifestations (arthritis, uveitis, skin lesions)
Risk Factors: family history, autoimmune predisposition, smoking (increases Crohn's risk), NSAIDs, previous GI infection, Western diet
Recommended Tests: colonoscopy with biopsy (gold standard), CRP, ESR, fecal calprotectin, CBC, albumin, B12, folate, iron studies, MRI enterography (Crohn's)
Treatment: 5-ASA, corticosteroids, immunomodulators (azathioprine, 6-MP), biologics (anti-TNF, vedolizumab, ustekinumab), surgery
Priority: High during flare, Medium for remission

NEUROLOGICAL DISEASES
======================

Migraine
Symptoms: unilateral throbbing headache (4-72 hours), nausea, vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia, aura (visual, sensory, speech disturbances)
Risk Factors: female gender, family history, hormonal fluctuations, stress, sleep deprivation, certain foods (tyramine, caffeine), dehydration
Recommended Tests: clinical diagnosis; CT/MRI brain (to rule out secondary causes), blood tests if new onset
Treatment: triptans (acute), NSAIDs, antiemetics, preventive (propranolol, topiramate, amitriptyline, CGRP antagonists)
Priority: Medium; High if thunder-clap headache (rule out SAH)

Stroke (Cerebrovascular Accident)
Symptoms: sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty (FAST acronym), sudden vision loss, severe headache, loss of balance, confusion
Risk Factors: hypertension (primary), atrial fibrillation, diabetes, smoking, hyperlipidemia, obesity, previous TIA, carotid stenosis, hypercoagulable states
Recommended Tests: urgent CT brain (no contrast to rule out hemorrhage), CT/MRI angiography, ECG, echocardiogram, CBC, coagulation studies, lipid panel, glucose
Treatment: tPA (within 4.5 hours for ischemic), thrombectomy, antiplatelet therapy, anticoagulation (AF-related), BP management
Priority: CRITICAL - time-sensitive emergency

Epilepsy
Symptoms: recurrent seizures (tonic-clonic, absence, focal), post-ictal confusion, tongue biting, urinary incontinence during seizure, aura
Risk Factors: brain injury, stroke, brain tumors, CNS infection, family history, developmental disorders, febrile seizures in childhood
Recommended Tests: EEG, MRI brain (with epilepsy protocol), blood glucose, electrolytes, toxicology screen, LP if meningitis suspected
Treatment: antiepileptic drugs (levetiracetam, valproate, lamotrigine, carbamazepine), ketogenic diet, vagal nerve stimulator, surgery
Priority: Critical for status epilepticus, High for new-onset

Multiple Sclerosis (MS)
Symptoms: fatigue, optic neuritis (visual loss/pain), motor weakness, sensory disturbances, bladder dysfunction, cognitive issues, balance problems, Lhermitte's sign
Risk Factors: female gender, age 20-40, Northern European ancestry, low vitamin D, smoking, previous EBV infection, family history
Recommended Tests: MRI brain and spine (McDonald criteria - T2 lesions), VEPs, CSF (oligoclonal bands, IgG index), blood tests to exclude alternatives
Treatment: disease-modifying therapies (interferon beta, glatiramer, natalizumab, ocrelizumab, siponimod), steroids for relapses, symptomatic management
Priority: High

RENAL DISEASES
==============

Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
Symptoms: early - often asymptomatic; late - fatigue, nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, swelling in legs/ankles, decreased urine output, hypertension, anemia symptoms, itching, cognitive changes
Risk Factors: diabetes (primary), hypertension (primary), glomerulonephritis, PKD, recurrent UTIs, NSAIDs overuse, contrast agents, family history
Recommended Tests: serum creatinine, eGFR (staging), BUN, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), urinalysis, CBC (anemia), electrolytes, phosphate, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, renal ultrasound
Treatment: BP control (target <130/80), ACE inhibitors/ARBs, glucose control, dietary protein/phosphate restriction, erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, dialysis (CKD stage 5), transplant
Priority: High for stages 4-5, Medium for stages 1-3

Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)
Symptoms: dysuria (painful urination), frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain, hematuria, cloudy/malodorous urine; pyelonephritis: flank pain, fever, rigors, nausea
Risk Factors: female gender, sexual activity, urinary catheter, urinary tract abnormalities, menopause, pregnancy, diabetes, immunosuppression, incomplete bladder emptying
Recommended Tests: urinalysis, urine culture and sensitivity, CBC (if pyelonephritis), renal ultrasound (if recurrent or complicated)
Treatment: trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, nitrofurantoin, fluoroquinolones (complicated), cephalosporins; IV antibiotics for pyelonephritis
Priority: High for pyelonephritis/urosepsis, Low for uncomplicated cystitis

INFECTIOUS DISEASES
===================

Influenza
Symptoms: sudden onset fever (38-40°C), headache, myalgia, malaise, dry cough, sore throat, rhinorrhea, fatigue; complications: pneumonia, myocarditis, encephalitis
Risk Factors: age > 65, age < 5, pregnancy, immunocompromised, chronic diseases, healthcare workers
Recommended Tests: rapid influenza diagnostic test (RIDT), RT-PCR (more sensitive), chest X-ray if complications suspected
Treatment: antivirals (oseltamivir, zanamivir) within 48 hours, supportive care, antipyretics; annual vaccination for prevention
Priority: High in elderly/immunocompromised, Medium in healthy adults

COVID-19
Symptoms: fever, dry cough, fatigue, loss of taste/smell (anosmia/ageusia), shortness of breath, body aches, sore throat, diarrhea; severe: pneumonia, ARDS, cytokine storm
Risk Factors: age > 65, obesity, diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, immunocompromised, smoking, CKD
Recommended Tests: RT-PCR (nasopharyngeal swab), rapid antigen test, chest CT (ground-glass opacities), CBC, CRP, ferritin, D-dimer, LFTs, ABG (severe)
Treatment: antivirals (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir for high-risk), dexamethasone (severe/critical with oxygen requirement), anticoagulation, oxygen therapy, remdesivir
Priority: Critical for severe, High for moderate

Tuberculosis (TB)
Symptoms: chronic cough > 3 weeks (productive, sometimes blood-tinged), night sweats, weight loss, fever, fatigue, chest pain; extrapulmonary: lymphadenopathy, meningitis, bone disease
Risk Factors: close contact with TB case, immunocompromised (HIV), malnutrition, diabetes, alcohol abuse, crowded living conditions, healthcare workers, travel to endemic areas
Recommended Tests: chest X-ray, sputum smear (AFB), sputum culture (gold standard), GeneXpert/MTB-RIF, IGRA (QuantiFERON), TST (Mantoux), HIV testing
Treatment: RIPE regimen (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) for 6 months; directly observed therapy (DOT)
Priority: High; Critical if MDR-TB or meningitis

HEMATOLOGICAL CONDITIONS
=========================

Iron Deficiency Anemia
Symptoms: fatigue, pallor, shortness of breath on exertion, palpitations, headache, dizziness, cold intolerance, restless legs, brittle nails, pica, koilonychia (spoon nails)
Risk Factors: blood loss (menstruation, GI bleeding), poor dietary iron intake, malabsorption (celiac disease, post-gastric surgery), pregnancy, rapid growth, celiac disease
Recommended Tests: CBC (low Hb, low MCV, low MCH), serum iron (low), TIBC (high), ferritin (low), peripheral blood smear (microcytic hypochromic), reticulocyte count, stool occult blood, endoscopy if GI source suspected
Treatment: oral iron supplementation (ferrous sulfate), IV iron (if intolerant or malabsorption), treat underlying cause, dietary iron-rich foods
Priority: High if severe (Hb < 8), Medium if mild-moderate

MENTAL HEALTH CONDITIONS
=========================

Major Depressive Disorder
Symptoms: depressed mood nearly every day, loss of interest/pleasure (anhedonia), changes in appetite/weight, sleep disturbances (insomnia/hypersomnia), fatigue, psychomotor changes, feelings of worthlessness/guilt, difficulty concentrating, recurrent thoughts of death/suicide
Risk Factors: family history, previous episodes, chronic illness, substance abuse, adverse childhood experiences, female gender, social isolation, major life stressors
Recommended Tests: clinical diagnosis (PHQ-9 scoring), thyroid function tests, CBC (rule out medical causes), vitamin B12, folate, vitamin D, substance use screening
Treatment: SSRIs (first-line), SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs, psychotherapy (CBT), ECT (severe), lifestyle modification
Priority: Critical if suicidal ideation, High otherwise

ONCOLOGY - COMMON CANCERS
==========================

Lung Cancer
Symptoms: chronic cough, hemoptysis, chest pain, dyspnea, unexplained weight loss, hoarseness, recurrent infections, bone pain, neurological symptoms (brain mets)
Risk Factors: smoking (primary, accounts for 85%), secondhand smoke, radon, asbestos, occupational exposures (arsenic, chromium), air pollution, family history, previous radiation to chest
Recommended Tests: chest CT (low-dose screening), bronchoscopy, CT-guided biopsy, PET scan, MRI brain, CBC, LFTs, LDH, molecular testing (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, PD-L1)
Treatment: surgery (early stage), chemotherapy, targeted therapy (EGFR inhibitors, ALK inhibitors), immunotherapy (PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors), radiation
Priority: High to Critical

Colorectal Cancer
Symptoms: change in bowel habits, rectal bleeding/blood in stool, abdominal pain/cramping, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, iron deficiency anemia, incomplete evacuation
Risk Factors: age > 50, personal/family history of CRC or polyps, IBD, hereditary syndromes (Lynch, FAP), obesity, sedentary lifestyle, red/processed meat diet, smoking, alcohol, diabetes
Recommended Tests: colonoscopy (gold standard), fecal occult blood test, fecal immunochemical test, CT colonography, CEA, CBC, LFTs, CT chest/abdomen/pelvis (staging)
Treatment: surgery (primary), adjuvant chemotherapy (FOLFOX, CAPOX), EGFR inhibitors (RAS wild-type), bevacizumab, immunotherapy (MSI-H)
Priority: High

COMMON LABORATORY REFERENCE RANGES
=====================================

Complete Blood Count (CBC):
- Hemoglobin: Men 13.5-17.5 g/dL, Women 12.0-15.5 g/dL
- Hematocrit: Men 41-53%, Women 36-46%
- WBC: 4,500-11,000/μL
- Platelets: 150,000-400,000/μL
- MCV: 80-100 fL

Metabolic Panel:
- Glucose (fasting): 70-100 mg/dL; Prediabetes: 100-125; Diabetes: ≥126
- HbA1c: Normal <5.7%; Prediabetes 5.7-6.4%; Diabetes ≥6.5%
- Creatinine: Men 0.7-1.2 mg/dL, Women 0.5-1.0 mg/dL
- eGFR: ≥90 (normal), 60-89 (mild), 30-59 (moderate), 15-29 (severe), <15 (kidney failure)
- Sodium: 135-145 mEq/L
- Potassium: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L
- BUN: 7-20 mg/dL
- ALT: 7-40 U/L
- AST: 10-40 U/L
- Total Bilirubin: 0.2-1.2 mg/dL
- Albumin: 3.5-5.0 g/dL

Lipid Panel:
- Total cholesterol: <200 mg/dL desirable
- LDL: <100 mg/dL optimal; <70 for very high risk
- HDL: Men >40 mg/dL, Women >50 mg/dL
- Triglycerides: <150 mg/dL normal; ≥500 risk for pancreatitis

Thyroid:
- TSH: 0.4-4.0 mIU/L
- Free T4: 0.8-1.8 ng/dL
- Free T3: 2.3-4.2 pg/mL

Cardiac Markers:
- Troponin I: <0.04 ng/mL (elevated in MI, myocarditis)
- BNP: <100 pg/mL normal; >400 likely heart failure
- CK-MB: <25 U/L

Inflammatory Markers:
- CRP: <1.0 mg/L (low risk); 1-3 mg/L (moderate); >3 mg/L (high risk)
- ESR: Men <15 mm/hr, Women <20 mm/hr
- Procalcitonin: <0.25 ng/mL (low bacterial infection risk)

PRIORITY LEVEL DEFINITIONS
============================

CRITICAL: Immediate medical attention required, life-threatening conditions
- Chest pain with ECG changes (MI), stroke symptoms, severe sepsis, anaphylaxis,
  DKA, pulmonary embolism, respiratory failure, suicidal ideation with plan

HIGH: Urgent evaluation needed, significant health risk within days
- Uncontrolled hypertension, new-onset severe symptoms, cancer concerns,
  significant anemia, worsening chronic disease

MEDIUM: Requires medical evaluation within days to weeks
- Stable chronic conditions requiring optimization, mild to moderate symptoms,
  preventive care, routine monitoring concerns

LOW: Routine follow-up appropriate within weeks to months
- Mild symptoms with low risk, preventive screenings, chronic disease maintenance
